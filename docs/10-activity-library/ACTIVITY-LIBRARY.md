# The Activity Library

*For one couple: Israel (UTC+3, works Sun-Thu) and New York City (UTC-4, works Mon-Fri). Seven hours apart. Generated 2026-08-02.*

---

## Read this before you use the tiers

**Tiering here is LOGISTICS ONLY.** Every activity was scored on how well it fits your clock, your energy at each hour, your two countries and your budget. **None of it is tuned to what either of you actually likes.** The taste profile had not arrived when this was built, so tier S means *fits your week best*, not *you'll enjoy this most*. That pass is built and waiting — see the hook in `build_library.py`.

**98 activities** — 89 verified, 9 plausible-but-unverified. 106 came in from research; 7 were merged away as duplicates and 1 dropped.

Two tiers of trust, kept deliberately separate:

- **verified** — the activity, the tool, or the protocol is traceable to official documentation, a store listing, a named clinician, or a peer-reviewed paper.
- **plausible-unverified** — reasonable, internally consistent, and each has a real source, but no strong precedent was found. Reddit was blocked for the whole research session, which is where these would normally be corroborated. **Treat them as hypotheses to try, not as practices with a track record.** They are never ranked alongside verified entries.

## How this library is actually shaped

Two patterns fell out of the research strongly enough to be worth stating. Both are countable from `library.json`, not impressions.

**1. One of you is nearly always the passive one — and that is the design, not a compromise.** 51 of 98 activities (52%) happen live and simultaneously but hand the two of you deliberately unequal roles: one narrates while the other listens with their eyes shut, one cooks while the other reads the recipe aloud, one holds the deck and reads, one rolls the dice and calls the numbers. The sleepy or busy partner is given the job that costs almost nothing. This is the single most common shape in the library and it showed up independently in the verbal games, the co-presence rituals, the card decks and the intimacy entries.

**2. Saturday is load-bearing, and it is a single point of failure.** 20 of the 24 activities that genuinely need both of you alert list Saturday as a window they work in. There is no weekday substitute — that is what 'the only symmetric window' means in practice. Everything demanding queues behind one day a week. Protect it, and don't schedule two heavy things into the same Saturday.

**What is NOT true, though it sounds like it should be:** that the answer to distance is to go asynchronous. Asynchronous activities are excellent and there are 23 of them — but they are a supplement, not the spine. Delete every asynchronous activity from this library and all nine windows still have content. Delete every live one and the library collapses to six of nine windows. Live, simultaneous, asymmetric-role activity is what actually holds the week together.
